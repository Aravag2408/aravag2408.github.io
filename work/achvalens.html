The problem

Achva LGBT reviews academic and professional texts for language that is outdated or harmful to LGBTQ+ people. Doing it by hand is slow, and different reviewers draw the line in different places, especially between "a bit outdated" and "actually harmful".

What I did

1

Turned their guidelines into data

We built a labeled list of 834 terms from Achva's guidelines, each marked fine, worth reconsidering, or harmful.

2

Split one hard decision into two easier ones

First: does this phrase need attention at all? Then, only for flagged phrases: should the writer reconsider it, or is it harmful? That is how a human reviewer thinks, and it made each step easier to check.

3

Let the computer tune the instructions

Instead of rewriting the AI's instructions by hand, I used an automated optimizer that tries many versions and keeps the ones that make fewer mistakes on the labeled terms.

4

Made it something people can use

A scanner where you paste text or upload a PDF or Word file, a downloadable audit report, and a reviewer dashboard where new terms get approved and fed back into the model.

The result

63.6% → 69.8%overall accuracy on 162 test terms
0.52 → 0.67F1 score for spotting harmful terms
81%accuracy on "needs attention or not"

The scanner: paste a text, get every relevant phrase marked green, yellow or red.

What I'd do next

The middle category, "worth reconsidering", is still the weakest. When I looked closer, the source list had 249 cases where the same word root got different labels. Cleaning that up would probably help more than any further tuning of the model.
