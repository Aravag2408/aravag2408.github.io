Mercury

A privacy audit of a medical chatbot. I showed that a stranger's private health details could end up in your answer, just by asking a similar question.

The problem

To sound more personal, some medical chatbots "remember" earlier conversations and use them as background when answering. Users assume what they share stays theirs. I asked a simple question: can one user's private details reach another user?

What I did

1

Set up a realistic chatbot

A sexual-health chatbot with login, where every conversation is stored in a shared memory the AI draws on.

2

Created 20 fictional patients

Each with a realistic and sensitive story: name, age, job, where they live, what they were worried about. All invented, no real people.

3

Played the attacker

From a separate account, I asked 20 ordinary questions, the kind any worried user would ask, and measured what came back at two levels: what reached the AI, and what it actually said.

4

Found the cause and the fixes

Traced the leak to a single missing check, then mapped each fix to the privacy principle it serves.

What I found

20 / 20questions pulled in other users' conversations
18 / 20patients' details reached the AI
6 / 20patients' details showed up in the reply

It also got worse over time. When a reply contained someone's details, that reply was saved to memory too, so the same person could leak again in a completely different conversation.

The cause: the search over past conversations never checked whose conversation it was. Fixing that is one line, but it is not enough on its own, so I recommended four layers:

1. Only search the user's own history. 2. Remove names and identifying details before storing anything (GDPR data minimisation and privacy by design). 3. Only store conversations users actively agreed to share, with the right to delete them (GDPR consent and erasure; Israeli Privacy Protection Law). 4. Scan replies for other people's details before sending them.

What I'd do next

Next I'd rerun the same 20 questions after each fix, to measure how much still leaks. Until a fix is tested that way, there's no proof it works.
