SugarBuddy and Beyond

I turned SugarBuddy's check-in into a benchmark dataset that pairs sensor-style data with a teen's free-text note, then made the method a reusable template and ran it on nine more pediatric conditions.

The code and paper are private.
My role
Everything, solo: the taxonomy, data generation, evaluation, and a check of every medical citation.

Stack

Who it's for

Researchers who need data where text and numbers both matter

Models that read a table and free text together need test data where both parts really carry signal. If a model can win by reading only the table, or only the text, the dataset isn't testing anything.

MulTaBench, the benchmark from my NLP course, makes that a hard rule: five tabular learners, four conditions each. A dataset counts only if the joint model beats both single-source baselines, and fine-tuning the text encoder beats a frozen one, for at least 3 of the 5 learners. The assignment was to build one dataset that passes. I built ten.

Key decisions

What I chose, and what it cost

Synthetic, but grounded in the literature

Chose
Generated data built on a 94-cause taxonomy of glucose anomalies, each cause backed by diabetes research.
Gave up
The realism of real patient records.
Why
Real data about minors isn't available or appropriate. Grounding every cause in research keeps the synthetic data honest.

Audit the text with a cheap probe

Chose
A simple TF-IDF classifier tries to guess the cause from the note alone. If it can, the note gives the answer away.
Gave up
Some speed: rows that leaked had to be rewritten and re-checked.
Why
It caught one difficulty tier that was accidentally too easy. Without the probe, the dataset would have passed for the wrong reason.

A template, not a one-off

Chose
Turned the method into steps anyone can repeat, and tested it on nine more conditions, each at 3 folds.
Gave up
Time I could have spent polishing a single dataset.
Why
One passing dataset could be luck. Ten show whether the method works, and where it breaks.
Under the hood

The template

Every medical claim matters here. The taxonomies cite 145 papers, and I checked each one against the source. 13 were overstated, mis-sourced or couldn't be confirmed, and I fixed every one before submitting.

Results

Ten datasets, five passed the full bar

5 / 5learners passed on SugarBuddy, well past the 3-of-5 bar
94causes in the glucose taxonomy, each grounded in research
145 / 145citations checked one by one; 13 fixed

The failures taught as much as the passes. Two datasets bracketed a U-shape: with too much signal in the table, the joint model saturates and fine-tuning has nothing to add; with too little, it fails the basic combined test. A fix found on a failing pilot, limiting how much the text encoder may adapt, later rescued the one weak learner in SugarBuddy itself.

What I'd do next

Make it more like the real world

An "unknown" label: every row has a definite cause today, but a real agent sometimes can't tell. I left it out on purpose to protect the margins; it's the honest next step.

Images too: the benchmark supports images alongside text and tables, and none of my ten datasets tried it yet.
